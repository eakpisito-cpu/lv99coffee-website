Matcha, honestly

Benefits and useful
matcha facts

Matcha contains interesting natural compounds, but we keep the claims grounded: it is not a treatment, a weight-loss promise, or a guarantee of the same result for everyone.

Naturally contains catechins

Green tea contains plant polyphenols, including the widely studied catechins. Actual amounts vary with the tea, production and drink recipe, so research on concentrated extracts should not be applied directly to one cup of matcha.

Contains caffeine

Matcha and green tea naturally contain caffeine, which may support alertness. Amounts and individual responses vary, and anyone sensitive to caffeine may prefer to avoid it close to bedtime.

A drink is not a supplement

EFSA considers green tea infusions and similar drinks generally safe for adults, while concentrated green tea extract supplements are a different context with different precautions. This page discusses café drinks, not supplements.

Milk and sweetness matter

Pure Matcha and Matcha Latte have different recipes and energy content. Milk and sweetness add energy, so guests managing sugar or dietary needs should choose the recipe and serving that suit them.

Sources: NCCIH: Green Tea · FDA: Caffeine · EFSA: Green Tea Catechins
This is general information, not medical advice.
